Linear Equations

A system of linear equations is a set of equations that share the same unknowns. In this lesson, you will learn how to write a system in matrix form and solve it using the inverse matrix. You will also explore how linear systems are used in computing.

What if there are more items?

Suppose the café sells four items: muffins \(x\), coffee \(y\), baked potatoes \(p\) and mushroom soup \(q\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 2x + 3y &= 8 \\ x + p &= 4 \\ y + q &= 6 \\ p + q &= 7 \end{aligned} \]

Elimination still works, but it takes many more steps. A delivery company may have thousands of unknowns, and one slip ruins every line after it. Matrices give one method that works for any number of unknowns: write the system as \(AX = b\), then \(X = A^{-1}b\). A computer can do this very quickly.

04

What a linear system is

30–40 min

A system of equations is a group of equations that are solved together. An equation is linear when each unknown has power 1. A system of \(n\) linear equations in \(n\) unknowns is an \(n \times n\) linear system.

One answer, no answer or many?

How many solutions does this system have?

More detail: the determinant tells you

A system with no solutions is called inconsistent. Write the system as \(AX = b\). If \(\det A \ne 0\), there is exactly one solution, \(X = A^{-1}b\). If \(\det A = 0\), \(A\) has no inverse, and the system has either no solution or infinitely many. The inverse method only works when \(\det A \ne 0\).

05

Writing a system as AX = b

40–50 min

Every linear system can be written as one matrix equation. \(A\) holds the numbers in front of the unknowns, \(X\) holds the unknowns and \(b\) holds the numbers on the right.

From equations to matrices

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} 2x + 3y &= 8 \\ 4x - y &= 2 \end{aligned} \) \( \longrightarrow \) \( \displaystyle \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & -1 \end{pmatrix}}_{A} \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}}_{X} = \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}}_{b} \)

Each row of \(A\) comes from one equation. Write the unknowns in the same order in every row. If an unknown is missing from an equation, its number in \(A\) is 0. A term such as \(-y\) gives \(-1\).

More detail: why \(X = A^{-1}b\)
\[ \begin{aligned} AX &= b \\ A^{-1}AX &= A^{-1}b \\ IX &= A^{-1}b \\ X &= A^{-1}b \end{aligned} \]

Multiply both sides by \(A^{-1}\) on the left. \(A^{-1}A = I\), and \(IX = X\). The order matters: \(A^{-1}b\), not \(bA^{-1}\).

06

Solving 2×2 systems

60–75 min

Watch the solution in Learn mode, or try the steps yourself in Practice mode.

The method

  1. Write the system as \(AX = b\).
  2. Find \(\det A\). If it is 0, stop: the inverse method cannot be used.
  3. Find \(A^{-1}\), as in Week 3.
  4. Multiply: \(X = A^{-1}b\).
  5. Check by putting your answers back into the equations.

09

Linear systems in computing

105–113 minApplications

Real problems often have several unknowns. Here are two that a linear system can solve.

1. Recommending songs

A listener rates two songs. Song A is 80% energetic and 20% acoustic, and gets 4 stars. Song B is 30% energetic and 70% acoustic, and gets 3 stars. Let \(x\) and \(y\) be how much the listener likes energetic and acoustic music.

\[ \begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 0.2 \\ 0.3 & 0.7 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} &= \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \\ \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} &= \begin{pmatrix} 4.4 \\ 2.4 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned} \]

2. Counting coffee sales

\[ \begin{aligned} x + y + z &= 60 \\ 3x + 4y + 5z &= 255 \\ y + z &= 40 \end{aligned} \]

A café sells small, medium and large coffees at $3, $4 and $5. One day it sold 60 coffees for $255, and 40 of them were medium or large. Let \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) be the numbers of small, medium and large coffees sold.

Optional reading: where else matrices are used

Neural networks store their weights in matrices and multiply them on graphics cards. Game engines keep each object's position, rotation and size in a transformation matrix. Search engines rank web pages using a very large matrix of links. Data scientists store data as matrices, with one row per item and one column per feature.
