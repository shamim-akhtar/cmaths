Vectors and Vector Operations

A vector describes a quantity that has both a size and a direction. Computing uses vectors everywhere: the position of a character in a game, the direction a camera is facing, the velocity of a moving object, and the colour of a pixel. In this lesson you will learn what a vector is, how to add and scale vectors, how to measure a vector's length, and how the dot product compares two directions.

01

What a vector is

0–8 min

A scalar has a size only. A vector has a size and a direction. A temperature of 20 °C is a scalar. A wind of 20 km/h blowing north is a vector, because the direction matters as much as the number.

Adding and subtracting vectors

To add two vectors, add the matching components. To subtract, subtract the matching components. The picture explains why: place the second arrow at the tip of the first, and the answer is the arrow from the start of the first to the tip of the second.

The same thing with numbers

Addition and subtraction work on one component at a time. The two vectors must have the same number of components.

\[ (a_1,\,a_2) + (b_1,\,b_2) = (a_1 + b_1,\; a_2 + b_2) \qquad (a_1,\,a_2) - (b_1,\,b_2) = (a_1 - b_1,\; a_2 - b_2) \]
\[ (3,\,1) + (1,\,3) = (3 + 1,\; 1 + 3) = (4,\,4) \qquad (3,\,1) - (1,\,3) = (3 - 1,\; 1 - 3) = (2,\,-2) \]
03

Scalar multiples

20–28 min

Multiplying a vector by a number multiplies every component by that number, which changes its length. A positive number keeps its direction. A negative number reverses its direction. Multiplying by zero gives the zero vector.

Dashed grey is the original vector
Length of \(\mathbf{a}\)3.61
Length of \(k\mathbf{a}\)7.21
DirectionUnchanged

The new length is the original length multiplied by the absolute value of \(k\). For example, multiplying by \(-2\) doubles the length and reverses the direction. Setting \(k\) to \(0\) gives the zero vector, which has no direction at all.

04

Magnitude and unit vectors

28–40 min

The magnitude of a vector is its length, written \(|\mathbf{a}|\). A unit vector has length 1. It shows the direction of the original vector, which is why graphics code uses unit vectors to describe which way something is facing.

Magnitude comes from Pythagoras

The two components form a right-angled triangle

The \(x\) component and the \(y\) component are the two short sides of a right-angled triangle. The vector itself is the hypotenuse, so its length comes straight from Pythagoras' theorem.

\[ |\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^{\,2} + a_2^{\,2}} \]
( , )

Shrinking a vector to length 1

The circle has radius 1

To find a unit vector, divide each component by the vector's length. The new vector has length 1 and points in the same direction. It is written with a hat, \(\hat{\mathbf{a}}\), and the step is called normalising.

\[ \hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{\mathbf{a}}{|\mathbf{a}|} \]

Check the answer by working out its magnitude. It should come to 1 every time. The one vector that cannot be normalised is the zero vector, because dividing by a length of zero is not defined.

05

The dot product

48–66 min

The dot product takes two vectors and gives back a single number, not a vector. It helps us compare the directions of two vectors, and its value can be positive, zero or negative. It is also the piece of this lesson that Tutorial 02 uses most, because matrix multiplication is built from it.

One pair at a time

Multiply the two \(x\) components together. Multiply the two \(y\) components together. Add the two results. That single number is the dot product.

\[ \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 \]

06

The angle between two vectors

66–78 min

Rearranging the formula above gives the angle itself. This is how a game works out whether an enemy is in front of the player or behind them, and how lighting decides how bright a surface should be.

\[ \cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}|\,|\mathbf{b}|} \qquad\qquad \theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left( \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}|\,|\mathbf{b}|} \right) \]

Work out the dot product first, then the two magnitudes, then divide. The answer is always between 0° and 180°.

07

Parallel, perpendicular and collinear

78–90 min

Let's learn how to check whether two vectors are parallel or perpendicular, and whether three points lie on the same straight line.

The three tests

How to check whether three points are collinear

Three points are collinear when they all lie on one straight line. You cannot tell by looking, so you test it with vectors. There are four steps.

  1. Pick one of the three points as the starting point. We will use \(P\).
  2. Find \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\). Subtract the coordinates of \(P\) from the coordinates of \(Q\).
  3. Find \(\overrightarrow{PR}\) the same way.
  4. Check whether one vector is a multiple of the other. If \(\overrightarrow{PR} = k\,\overrightarrow{PQ}\) for some number \(k\), the three points are collinear.
\[ \overrightarrow{PQ} = (q_1 - p_1,\; q_2 - p_2) \qquad \overrightarrow{PR} = (r_1 - p_1,\; r_2 - p_2) \]

Both vectors must start from the same point. Two parallel vectors that start from different points do not make the three points collinear.

Vectors in three dimensions

90–100 minOptional application

A 3D vector has three components: \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\). We can use the same methods to find its length and its dot product. There is simply one more component to carry.

Throughout, \(\mathbf{p} = (4,\,3)\), \(\mathbf{q} = (-2,\,6)\) and \(\mathbf{r} = (1,\,-5)\).

1.Find the magnitude \(|\mathbf{p}|\).
\( |\mathbf{p}| = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \). Square each component, add them, then take the square root.
2.Find \(\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q}\).

\( \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q} = (4 + (-2),\; 3 + 6) = (2,\,9) \). Add the matching components.
3.Find \(3\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{q}\).
( , )
\( 3\mathbf{p} = (12,\,9) \), so \( 3\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{q} = (12 - (-2),\; 9 - 6) = (14,\,3) \). Multiply every component of \(\mathbf{p}\) by 3 first, then subtract the matching components of \(\mathbf{q}\).
4.Find the dot product \(\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{q}\).
\( \mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{q} = (4)(-2) + (3)(6) = -8 + 18 = 10 \). Multiply the matching components, then add the results.
5.Find the unit vector \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}\).
( , )
\( \hat{\mathbf{p}} = \dfrac{\mathbf{p}}{|\mathbf{p}|} = \dfrac{1}{5}(4,\,3) = (0.8,\,0.6) \). Divide each component by the magnitude found in question 1.
6.Find the angle between \(\mathbf{p}\) and \(\mathbf{q}\), to the nearest degree.
°
\( |\mathbf{q}| = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{40} \approx 6.325 \), so \( \cos\theta = \dfrac{10}{5 \times 6.325} \approx 0.3162 \) and \( \theta \approx 72^\circ \). Find the dot product and both magnitudes, then divide.
7.Find \(m\) so that \((m,\,4)\) is perpendicular to \(\mathbf{p}\).
Perpendicular means the dot product is zero: \( (4)(m) + (3)(4) = 0 \), so \( 4m + 12 = 0 \) and \( m = -3 \). Write the dot product out and solve for \(m\).
8.Find \(n\) so that \((8,\,n)\) is parallel to \(\mathbf{p}\).
Parallel means one vector is a multiple of the other: \( (8,\,n) = k(4,\,3) \). The first components give \( 8 = 4k \), so \( k = 2 \), and then \( n = 3k = 6 \). Both components must give the same \(k\).

Question 9, in three dimensions

Extended 3D question

Let \(\mathbf{u} = (1,\,2,\,2)\) and \(\mathbf{v} = (3,\,0,\,4)\). This question uses vectors with three components instead of two. The calculation steps are the same. You just need to include the third component.

9(a).Find \(|\mathbf{u}|\).
\( |\mathbf{u}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = \sqrt{9} = 3 \). Square all three components and add them, then find the square root.
9(b).Find \(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}\).
\( \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = (1)(3) + (2)(0) + (2)(4) = 3 + 0 + 8 = 11 \). Multiply the three pairs of matching components, then add the results.
9(c).Find the angle between \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\), to the nearest degree.
°
\( |\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{9 + 0 + 16} = 5 \), so \( \cos\theta = \dfrac{11}{3 \times 5} = \dfrac{11}{15} \approx 0.7333 \) and \( \theta \approx 43^\circ \). Find both magnitudes, then use the dot product formula.
9(d).Find \(t\) so that \((t,\,3,\,-1)\) is perpendicular to \(\mathbf{u}\).
The dot product is zero because the vectors are perpendicular: \( (1)(t) + (2)(3) + (2)(-1) = 0 \), so \( t + 6 - 2 = 0 \) and \( t = -4 \). Check it: \( -4 + 6 - 2 = 0 \).
9(e).The points \(A(1,\,0,\,2)\), \(B(3,\,2,\,5)\) and \(C(7,\,k,\,11)\) are collinear. Find \(k\).
\( \overrightarrow{AB} = (2,\,2,\,3) \) and \( \overrightarrow{AC} = (6,\,k,\,9) \). Collinear means \( \overrightarrow{AC} = m\,\overrightarrow{AB} \). The first components give \( 6 = 2m \), so \( m = 3 \), and the third components agree because \( 9 = 3 \times 3 \). The second components then give \( k = 3 \times 2 = 6 \). All three components must give the same multiplier.
